Manaswi Raghurama

Ecologist | Restoration | Invasive Species

I am an ecologist, currently working as a postdoctoral researcher with the SNAPP-MoRe working group and the Missouri Botanical Garden, USA. I am working to simplify the monitoring of restoration outcomes in tropical forests, savannas, and mangroves. A core part of my current work is to synthesize existing long-term monitoring datasets from around the tropics to investigate whether short-term monitoring can predict long-term outcomes of restoration. I am also characterizing existing monitoring “resources” (standards, protocols, frameworks, guidelines, etc.) to build an online tool for practitioners to find potential monitoring “resources” that apply to their requirements.

Previously, during my PhD, I studied various community- and ecosystem-level impacts of woody invasive plants in a high-altitude forest-grassland mosaic in the Western Ghats biodiversity hotspot in south India. I studied the impacts of invasive plants on soil inorganic Nitrogen cycling, export of nutrients and sediments in stream water, and the native grassland community.

I am interested in socio-ecological research, and how it can be used to evaluate social, environmental, and policy programs to design better interventions. I co-led the writing of a report aimed at guiding the restoration of terrestrial ecosystems in India - a product of several stakeholders meeting as a part of the preparatory phase of India’s National Mission on Biodiversity and Human Well-Being.
