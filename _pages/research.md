<p>
My research integrates empirical field ecology, data synthesis, statistical analysis, and socio-ecological research to:<br>
    • investigate how ecosystems respond to anthropogenic activities and biological invasions,<br>
    • to build tools that help increase the effectiveness of restoration and conservation practitioners, and<br>
    • to build better environmental policies.
</p> 

<div class="row mt-5">
  <div class="col-sm-12">
    <h3>1. Restoration Ecology</h3>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="row">
  <div class="col-sm-6 mt-3 mt-md-0">
    {% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/2_Tropical Forest Restoration Scientists Workshop_Nathan Kwarta_05142026.JPG" title="SNAPP MoRe working group" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}
  </div>
  <div class="col-sm-6 mt-3 mt-md-0">
    <p>
      As the postdoctoral researcher in the <a href="https://snapp-more.github.io/" target="_blank">SNAPP-MoRe working group</a>, I am working to simplify restoration monitoring for practitioners in the tropics. Previously based in the University of Minnesota, and currently with the Missouri Botanical Garden, our working group's aims to help practitioners plan their monitoring efforts, and reach out to global NGOs with recommendations (backed by empirical evidence) for monitoring. We are synthesizing information from restoration at various scales, from plots to landscapes, towards this purpose. 
    </p>
    <p>
      We have three ongoing inter-related projects:<br>
      • Synthesizing long-term monitoring datasets to evaluate whether short-term indicators can reliably predict long-term ecological restoration outcomes across tropical forests, savannas, and mangroves.<br>
      • Summarizing and characterizing existing monitoring "resources" - documents like standards, protocols, frameworks, guidelines, etc. - to build an online tool where practitioners can put in their project details and monitoring requirements to get potential monitoring "resources" that they can use.<br>
      • Conducting an online questionnaire of > 200 restoration and conservation practitioners in the tropics to understand how practitioners are currently monitoring the outcomes of their projects.
    </p>
  </div>
</div>

    <h3>2. Plant Invasions</h3>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="row">
  <div class="col-sm-6 mt-3 mt-md-0">
    {% include figure.liquid loading="eager" path="assets/img/2_Cover image.JPG" title="Invaded Shola-grassland mosaic" class="img-fluid rounded z-depth-1" %}
  </div>
  <div class="col-sm-6 mt-3 mt-md-0">
    <p>
      During my PhD, I studied various community- and ecosystem-level impacts of woody invasive species on shola (forest)-grassland mosaics in the high-altitudes of the Nilgiris Biosphere Reserve, a part of the Western Ghats global biodiversity hotspot. I specifically focused on the impacts of woody invasives on soil inorganic nitrogen cycling, stream nutrient and sediment export, and grassland community. I then integrated this with knowledge from similar forest-grassland mosaics around the tropics, to design a conceptual framework to guide the restoration of these heavily-invaded ecosystems.
    </p>

  <div class="col-sm-6 mt-3 mt-md-0">
    <p>
      I am interested in synthesizing existing ecological knowledge to design more robust environmental policies. I was one of the co-leads in the design and writing of a report to help guide the restoration of terrestrial ecosystems. I was also involved in the writing of a policy report on invasive alien species in India, with contributions on the state-of-the-art regarding the impacts on ecosystem functioning and biogeochemical cycles. Both of these reports were prepared as a part of the preparatory phase of India's National Mission on Biodiversity and Human Well-Being.

<div class="row">

  <div class="col-sm-6 mt-3 mt-md-0">
    <p>
      I am interested in conducting socio-ecological research to understand human-environment interactions, with the aim to gain insights that help design restoration and conservation interventions which do not negatively impact human societies. Before my PhD, I worked as a research fellow for 2 months with the <a href="https://www.panchabhuta.org/" target="_blank">Panchabhuta Conservation Foundation</a>, conducting surveys in the Aghanashini river estuary area to understand how the local farming and fishing communities use natural resources in the region. After my PhD, I also worked as a consultant for 6 months with the <a href="https://centreforpastoralism.org/" target="_blank">Centre for Pastoralism</a>, preparing a report on the penning economy in central India - a practice in which sheep and other livestock (mainly, sheep and goats) are made to stay in a temporarily fenced piece (a “pen”) of farmland.
